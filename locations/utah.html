Climate & HVAC Demands in Utah

Utah spans IECC Zones 3B, 5B, and 6B. With summer highs averaging 88°F, cooling is the primary demand on your HVAC system. Winter lows near 18°F mean heating needs are moderate but still important. Average electricity rates sit at 13.75¢/kWh, making system efficiency an important factor in your monthly energy costs. Learn more about furnace options for your home.

SEER2 Efficiency Requirements in Utah

Utah falls within the North SEER2 region. As of January 2023, all new central air conditioning systems installed in Utah must meet a minimum SEER2 rating of 13.4. When replacing your AC or heat pump, ensure your contractor installs a unit that meets or exceeds this minimum — upgrading to a higher-efficiency system can significantly reduce your energy bills. See our summer AC guide for more on efficiency ratings.

HVAC Licensing Requirements in Utah

Utah requires HVAC contractors to hold a license issued by the Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL) — S350 HVAC License. Before hiring, verify your contractor's credentials through the board's license lookup tool. Licensed contractors carry insurance, pull permits correctly, and stand behind their work. See our safety tips for more on what to verify before hiring.

Utility Companies & HVAC Rebates in Utah

The major utility providers in Utah include Rocky Mountain Power (PacifiCorp), Dominion Energy Utah, Murray Power.

Available rebate programs:

  • Wattsmart Rebates
  • Dominion ThermWise Rebates
  • Federal Tax Credits — may apply for qualifying high-efficiency installations; check energystar.gov for current amounts and eligibility

Contact your utility provider or HVAC contractor to confirm current eligibility and amounts. Learn about HVAC financing options.

HVAC Installation Costs in Utah

A standard central AC replacement in Utah typically costs $3,500–$7,000, while furnace installations run $3,200–$7,000. Costs vary by city, system size, efficiency rating, and installation complexity. Higher-efficiency units cost more upfront but lower monthly bills, especially given Utah's electricity rates.
