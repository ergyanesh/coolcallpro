Toledo Climate by the Numbers

Toledo's climate is heating-dominated: NOAA's 1991-2020 Climate Normals record 6,145 annual heating degree days against just 793 cooling degree days — a 7.7-to-1 heating-to-cooling load. In an average year, 14.3 days per year hit 90°F — short hot stretches rather than sustained heat and 121.9 days drop below freezing — cold-climate heat pump capacity matters here, and gas furnace reliability is non-negotiable. Annual snowfall averages 37.6" per year, enough that outdoor units need clearance from snowdrifts and gutter runoff.

Toledo Housing Stock & HVAC Considerations

Toledo's housing stock is among the oldest in Ohio: per the U.S. Census ACS 5-year estimates, the median home was built in 1955 — 71 years old on average. Original ductwork from this era often leaks 25-35% of conditioned air through joint failures, and many homes still have undersized electrical panels. Natural gas heat dominates: 73.9% of homes burn utility gas and only 23.6% heat with electricity. Heat pump conversions face cheap-gas economics that often favor staying with a high-efficiency furnace. Owner-occupancy runs 52.7% with a modest median home value of $107,000 — replacement budgets tend to favor reliable mid-tier equipment over premium-tier upgrades.

Heating Operating Cost in Toledo: Heat Pump vs Gas Furnace

At Toledo's state-average rates (17.5¢/kWh electricity, $1.33/therm natural gas), the gas furnace wins on operating cost: $17.12/MMBtu for the heat pump vs $13.83/MMBtu for the gas furnace, a 19% margin. For a typical 1,500 sqft home in Toledo's climate (6,145 HDD), that maps to roughly $1,150/year for a heat pump vs $950/year for a gas furnace at current rates — actual usage varies with insulation, thermostat habits, and equipment efficiency. The math, transparently: 1 MMBtu = 293.07 kWh = 10 therms. Heat pump cost-per-MMBtu = (electricity rate × 293.07) ÷ COP; gas furnace cost-per-MMBtu = (gas rate × 10) ÷ AFUE. Modern heat pumps achieve a coefficient of performance (COP) of 2.5 to 4 across most U.S. climates per the U.S. Department of Energy; modern gas furnaces hit 95-98% AFUE.

FirstEnergy Toledo Edison Rebates — Limited Status

Toledo’s electric service is provided by Toledo Edison (a FirstEnergy Ohio company). After a comprehensive efficiency-program suspension in 2020 pursuant to PUCO order, the FirstEnergy Ohio utilities resumed a residential products rebate program from June 2023 through May 2024 that included a $500 heat pump water heater rebate but did not include central air-source heat pump rebates. As of early 2026 the only active residential incentives are a smart-thermostat rebate and the Community Connections demand-response program. FirstEnergy filed its sixth Electric Security Plan with PUCO in January 2025 proposing new residential efficiency programs — if approved, central HVAC rebates may return. Ohio’s state-administered HEAR & HOMES programs were awarded $249 million in federal Inflation Reduction Act funding but have not launched as of early 2026 — the Ohio Department of Development is finalizing program design. When live, HEAR will offer up to $8,000 for a qualifying heat pump for income-eligible households.

Ohio HVAC Contractor Licensing (OCILB)

HVAC work in Toledo is regulated at the state level by the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board (OCILB). The HVAC contractor application fee is $50 per classification; applicants must carry at least $500,000 in liability insurance with OCILB listed as the certificate holder, plus workers’ compensation if employees are involved. The state HVAC contractor exam is administered by PSI; passing requires 70% on both the trade and the business & law sections. Licenses renew biennially with a mandatory 8 hours of annual continuing education. State-level OCILB licensure does not require a surety bond, but Toledo and surrounding Lucas County jurisdictions may impose local bond requirements separate from the state licence. Local permit pulls run through the City of Toledo Division of Building Inspection. Anyone handling refrigerant in the course of HVAC service must also hold a current EPA Section 608 certification under federal law; verify that OCILB licence number and EPA-608 status before signing.

Federal Tax Credits — Status After OBBBA (2026)

The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (IRS Section 25C) was terminated for property placed in service after Dec 31, 2025 by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025). HVAC equipment installed in 2026 does not qualify for the federal credit. Homeowners whose equipment was installed by Dec 31, 2025 may still claim the credit on their 2025 tax return. For 2026 installations, check the ENERGY STAR Rebate Finder and the state programs described above.
