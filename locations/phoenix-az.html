❄️ NO AC

AC out, blowing warm, or short-cycling

Outdoor unit silent · indoor blower running but warm air · system short-cycling on/off in 100°F+ heat. In Phoenix's extreme heat, an AC failure becomes a habitability code issue (Phoenix City Code 39-5) within hours — the most common culprits are electrical (capacitor, contactor, low refrigerant) and require a technician.

⚠️ STRANGE NOISES

Banging, screaming, or grinding outdoor unit

Loud bangs · metal-on-metal screaming · grinding from the condenser. In Phoenix summers your outdoor unit runs at near-100% capacity for hours — failing fan motors, compressor bearings, and warped fan blades are common. Turn the system off and call before damage spreads to the compressor itself.

🔥 NO HEAT

Furnace or heat pump not heating

Furnace won't ignite · heat pump blowing cold air on a 40°F desert night · short-cycling. Phoenix's heating season is short (Dec–Feb) but cold snaps still happen. If you smell gas, leave the building immediately and call 911 first.

📋 ROC R-39 License

Arizona Registrar of Contractors

Per the Arizona ROC License Classifications, the residential HVAC class is R-39 Air Conditioning and Refrigeration, Including Solar — covering installation and repair of comfort AC, refrigeration, evaporative cooling, ventilating, and heating with or without solar equipment. Dual-scope CR-39 covers commercial too. Verify the license number before authorizing any work.

💰 Local Rebates & Permits

APS, SRP, Phoenix Permitting

Phoenix is served by either APS or Salt River Project (SRP) depending on territory. SRP HVAC rebates typically run $75–$1,125/ton for qualifying high-efficiency equipment (program details subject to ACC Decision No. 81584; verify before assuming amounts). Mechanical permit fees are valuation-based per the City of Phoenix Planning & Development Fee Schedule (Appendix A.2).
