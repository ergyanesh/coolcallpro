Kansas City Climate by the Numbers

NOAA's 1991-2020 Climate Normals record 5,133 heating degree days and 1,360 cooling degree days for Kansas City — a heating-leaning climate where furnaces and heat pumps run more hours than central AC. In an average year, 32.6 days per year top 90°F, putting consistent strain on central AC equipment through summer and 104.3 days fall below 32°F, requiring competent heating capacity through a real winter season. Snowfall is modest at 18.8" per year — occasional accumulation rather than a structural design factor.

Heating Operating Cost in Kansas City: Heat Pump vs Gas Furnace

At Kansas City's state-average rates (12.2¢/kWh electricity, $1.63/therm natural gas), the heat pump wins on operating cost: $11.89/MMBtu for the heat pump vs $16.94/MMBtu for the gas furnace, a 30% margin. For a typical 1,500 sqft home in Kansas City's climate (5,133 HDD), that maps to roughly $700/year for a heat pump vs $950/year for a gas furnace at current rates — actual usage varies with insulation, thermostat habits, and equipment efficiency. The math, transparently: 1 MMBtu = 293.07 kWh = 10 therms. Heat pump cost-per-MMBtu = (electricity rate × 293.07) ÷ COP; gas furnace cost-per-MMBtu = (gas rate × 10) ÷ AFUE. Modern heat pumps achieve a coefficient of performance (COP) of 2.5 to 4 across most U.S. climates per the U.S. Department of Energy; modern gas furnaces hit 95-98% AFUE.

Dual-Load Climate Considerations (Zone 4A)

Per U.S. Department of Energy Energy Saver guidance, Zone 4A mixed-humid climates like Kansas City produce both meaningful winter heating loads and significant summer latent (moisture) loads. The 1,707.6 CDD combined with 38.13 inches of precipitation means cooling-season dehumidification is a real design concern, while 4,613.0 HDD requires reliable heating capacity through Missouri’s documented sub-zero cold snaps.
