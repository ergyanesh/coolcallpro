Climate & HVAC Demands in Montana

Montana spans IECC Zones 6B and 7. With winter lows dropping to 8°F, heating is the primary demand on your HVAC system. Summer highs near 81°F still require reliable cooling. Average electricity rates sit at 14.33¢/kWh, making system efficiency an important factor in your monthly energy costs. Learn more about furnace options for your home.

SEER2 Efficiency Requirements in Montana

Montana falls within the North SEER2 region. As of January 2023, all new central air conditioning systems installed in Montana must meet a minimum SEER2 rating of 13.4. When replacing your AC or heat pump, ensure your contractor installs a unit that meets or exceeds this minimum — upgrading to a higher-efficiency system can significantly reduce your energy bills. See our summer AC guide for more on efficiency ratings.

HVAC Licensing Requirements in Montana

Montana requires HVAC contractors to hold a license issued by the Dept. of Labor and Industry (contractor registration, not HVAC-specific). Before hiring, verify your contractor's credentials through the board's license lookup tool. Licensed contractors carry insurance, pull permits correctly, and stand behind their work. See our safety tips for more on what to verify before hiring.

Utility Companies & HVAC Rebates in Montana

The major utility providers in Montana include NorthWestern Energy, Montana-Dakota Utilities, Flathead Electric Coop.

Available rebate programs:

  • NorthWestern Energy EE Rebates
  • MT DEQ Weatherization
  • Federal Tax Credits — may apply for qualifying high-efficiency installations; check energystar.gov for current amounts and eligibility

Contact your utility provider or HVAC contractor to confirm current eligibility and amounts. Learn about HVAC financing options.

HVAC Installation Costs in Montana

A furnace replacement in Montana typically costs $3,200–$7,500, and AC replacements run $3,500–$7,000. Costs vary by city, system size, efficiency rating, and installation complexity. Higher-efficiency units cost more upfront but lower monthly bills, especially given Montana's electricity rates. See our full HVAC cost guide for detailed pricing breakdowns.
