Arizona climate, efficiency code & typical costs

🌡️ Climate Demands

Cooling-dominated, hot-dry desert

Arizona spans IECC Zones 2B, 3B, 4B, 5B. Summer highs avg 98°F, winter lows near 32°F. Average electricity 15.62¢/kWh — system efficiency directly drives monthly costs. Most homes use heat pumps for efficient year-round comfort. Learn more about heat pump options.

💡 SEER2 Code

Southwest region: SEER2 14.3 + EER2 11.7

As of January 2023, all new central AC systems in Arizona must meet SEER2 14.3 with the additional EER2 11.7 peak-load standard for the Southwest hot-dry region. Higher-efficiency systems pay back faster given Arizona's extreme cooling load. See our summer AC guide.

💰 Typical Costs

Arizona HVAC installation costs

Standard central AC replacement: $4,000–$8,500. Furnace installation: $2,500–$5,500. Costs vary by city, system size, and efficiency. See our full HVAC cost guide.

🌀️ Climate Hazards

Extreme heat, dust storms, wildfire

Per FEMA's National Risk Index, Arizona faces elevated extreme heat, dust storm, and wildfire exposure. Dust scours coil fins; wildfire smoke clogs filters within days. MERV 13 filters during smoke events; coil cleaning before each cooling season.

📋 Licensing Requirements

Arizona ROC R-39 License

Arizona requires HVAC contractors to hold a license issued by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC). The residential HVAC classification is R-39 Air Conditioning and Refrigeration, Including Solar. Before hiring, verify your contractor's credentials through the AZ ROC license lookup tool.

Common HVAC issues in Arizona

Why Call a Arizona HVAC Pro

Four facts from climate and licensing data specific to Arizona.

01
🌡️

Climate Load

Arizona averages 98°F summer highs and 32°F winter lows, with 3,800 annual cooling degree days and 1,350 heating degree days. A mis-sized or poorly-charged system fails early under that load.

02
📝

Licensing

Arizona requires HVAC contractors to hold a state license from the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Unlicensed work can void manufacturer warranties and complicate insurance claims.

03
💡

Efficiency Code

New central AC units installed in Arizona must meet SEER2 14.3 (Southwest SEER2 region) under the federal efficiency standard effective January 1, 2023. A pro ensures the installed system is compliant and sized correctly.

04
🌀️

Weather Risks

FEMA's National Risk Index identifies Extreme Heat, Dust Storm, and Wildfire as elevated hazards in Arizona. A technician inspects outdoor equipment for damage after any major weather event before restart.
