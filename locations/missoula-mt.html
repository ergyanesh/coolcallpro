Missoula Climate by the Numbers

Missoula's climate is heating-dominated: NOAA's 1991-2020 Climate Normals record 7,283 annual heating degree days against just 331 cooling degree days — a 22.0-to-1 heating-to-cooling load. In an average year, 25.4 days per year hit 90°F — short hot stretches rather than sustained heat and 164.4 days drop below freezing — cold-climate heat pump capacity matters here, and gas furnace reliability is non-negotiable. Annual snowfall averages 37.9" per year, enough that outdoor units need clearance from snowdrifts and gutter runoff.

Heating Operating Cost in Missoula: Heat Pump vs Gas Furnace

At Missoula's state-average rates (13.3¢/kWh electricity, $0.79/therm natural gas), the gas furnace wins on operating cost: $13.02/MMBtu for the heat pump vs $8.26/MMBtu for the gas furnace, a 37% margin. For a typical 1,500 sqft home in Missoula's climate (7,283 HDD), that maps to roughly $1,050/year for a heat pump vs $650/year for a gas furnace at current rates — actual usage varies with insulation, thermostat habits, and equipment efficiency. The math, transparently: 1 MMBtu = 293.07 kWh = 10 therms. Heat pump cost-per-MMBtu = (electricity rate × 293.07) ÷ COP; gas furnace cost-per-MMBtu = (gas rate × 10) ÷ AFUE. Modern heat pumps achieve a coefficient of performance (COP) of 2.5 to 4 across most U.S. climates per the U.S. Department of Energy; modern gas furnaces hit 95-98% AFUE.

Mountain Valley Cold-Dry Climate (Zone 6B)

Missoula’s semi-arid 14.11-inch precipitation means whole-house humidification is a standard comfort investment during the heating season.
