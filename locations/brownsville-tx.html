Heating Operating Cost in Brownsville: Heat Pump vs Gas Furnace

At Brownsville's state-average rates (15.4¢/kWh electricity, $1.73/therm natural gas), the heat pump wins on operating cost: $15.05/MMBtu for the heat pump vs $18.06/MMBtu for the gas furnace, a 17% margin. For a typical 1,500 sqft home in Brownsville's climate (554 HDD), that maps to roughly $100/year for a heat pump vs $100/year for a gas furnace at current rates — actual usage varies with insulation, thermostat habits, and equipment efficiency. The math, transparently: 1 MMBtu = 293.07 kWh = 10 therms. Heat pump cost-per-MMBtu = (electricity rate × 293.07) ÷ COP; gas furnace cost-per-MMBtu = (gas rate × 10) ÷ AFUE. Modern heat pumps achieve a coefficient of performance (COP) of 2.5 to 4 across most U.S. climates per the U.S. Department of Energy; modern gas furnaces hit 95-98% AFUE.

Extreme Cooling Dominance & Hurricane Exposure (Zone 2A)

Distinctive: Brownsville PUB (Municipal Electric Utility)

Brownsville is served by the Brownsville Public Utilities Board (PUB) — a city-owned municipal electric utility. This means Brownsville electric customers contract with the City directly rather than with a private investor-owned utility or a deregulated retail electricity provider. The PUB administers its own residential HVAC rebate program; contact Brownsville PUB directly for the current heat pump and central AC rebate amounts.

Texas HVAC Contractor Licensing (TDLR)

Texas requires HVAC contractors to hold an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor License issued by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). The TDLR administers examinations and issues licenses for ACR contracting statewide. Verify a specific contractor’s current TDLR license status before contracting. Permit-pulling responsibility typically rests with the licensed installing contractor.
