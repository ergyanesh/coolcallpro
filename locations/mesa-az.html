Heating Operating Cost in Mesa: Heat Pump vs Gas Furnace

At Mesa's state-average rates (16.0¢/kWh electricity, $1.97/therm natural gas), the heat pump wins on operating cost: $15.66/MMBtu for the heat pump vs $20.56/MMBtu for the gas furnace, a 24% margin. For a typical 1,500 sqft home in Mesa's climate (935 HDD), that maps to roughly $150/year for a heat pump vs $200/year for a gas furnace at current rates — actual usage varies with insulation, thermostat habits, and equipment efficiency. The math, transparently: 1 MMBtu = 293.07 kWh = 10 therms. Heat pump cost-per-MMBtu = (electricity rate × 293.07) ÷ COP; gas furnace cost-per-MMBtu = (gas rate × 10) ÷ AFUE. Modern heat pumps achieve a coefficient of performance (COP) of 2.5 to 4 across most U.S. climates per the U.S. Department of Energy; modern gas furnaces hit 95-98% AFUE.

Federal SEER2 Minimum & Attic-Duct Efficiency

Per the U.S. Department of Energy Energy Saver guide on Central Air Conditioning, federal DOE rulemaking under 10 CFR Part 430 (effective January 1, 2023) requires Southern-region central air conditioners — which includes Arizona — to meet a minimum SEER2 14.3 rating for split-system units under 45,000 BTU/hr (equivalent to SEER 15, higher than the Northern-region SEER2 13.4 floor). For Mesa homeowners replacing equipment, that’s the regulatory minimum, not a recommendation. DOE further advises that “duct losses can account for more than 30% of energy consumption for space conditioning, especially if the ducts are in an unconditioned space such as an attic” — directly relevant to Mesa’s 1988-median housing stock where most supply ducts run through vented attics that exceed 140°F during summer afternoons.
